June 18, 2026

Commission Approves Part One Expansion Permit for Submission Permit

The Commission voted unanimously to approve the Part One horizontal expansion permit application for submission to TDEC. Part One identifies expansion property limits, buffers, and regional location; it does not include geotechnical or hydrogeologic studies, which follow in Part Two. The borrow pit plan was also presented, with roughly 19.7 acres of influenced area requiring stormwater management, and Republic committed to maintaining the borrow pit property in perpetuity. The most recent TDEC inspection at that point was characterized as clean with no violations.

Source LCSWDC Approved Minutes, June 18, 2026 (PDF)
July 16, 2026

TDEC Issues Two Areas of Concern; Borrow Pit Plan Approved Compliance

Republic reported two Areas of Concern (AOCs) in the most recent TDEC inspector report: exposed waste and failure to adhere to final cover requirements. TDEC gave the Commission and Republic 30 days to clear the AOCs and required a directive plan. Republic stated the landfill’s permit assumes soil availability that is not on hand, and that it was hauling roughly 400 tons of soil per week to compensate. The Commission approved the borrow pit plan unanimously and took up the survey of the 7-acre and 15,000-square-foot parcels.

Source LCSWDC Approved Minutes, July 16, 2026 (PDF)
August 20, 2026

AOC Directive Plan and Expansion Permit Status on the Agenda Meeting

The August agenda carried the AOC directive plan for exposed waste and final cover, the horizontal expansion permit submission status, and the property surplus declaration and realtor RFQ. Public comment raised groundwater near residential wells, an outstanding public records request, and vulture control. Approved minutes for this meeting had not been published by the county as of September 18, 2026; the linked packet is the primary record currently available.

Source LCSWDC Packet, August 20, 2026 (PDF, 42 MB, loudoncounty-tn.gov)
September 17, 2026

Part One Submitted; AOCs Recur and TDEC Asks the County to Respond Permit

Republic reported that Part One of the horizontal expansion permit has been submitted, with geotechnical investigation ongoing and expected to continue into early 2027 and water quality work to follow. The TDEC inspector was on site in both July and August and issued Areas of Concern for exposed waste and east-slope flagging tied to soil availability. TDEC asked that the permit holder — Loudon County, through the Commission — formally acknowledge the condition, since the County holds the permit even though Republic operates the site. The groundwater monitoring report was submitted August 14, 2026. The vulture control contract was reported ineffective and will not continue. A $6,800 legal fee payment was approved unanimously. Ms. Coffey’s public records request has been outstanding more than 60 days.

Source LCSWDC Packet and Minutes, September 17, 2026 (PDF)
September 17, 2026

Commission Chair Resigns; New Chair Elected the Same Night Leadership

The meeting opened with the announcement that notice had been received the previous day, September 16, 2026, that Adam Waller had resigned from the Solid Waste Disposal Commission. Waller held two roles at once: chair of the Commission and the person in charge of its public records. Commissioner Ross was nominated and elected chair the same night, on a motion by Gary and a second by Tyler, with Ross abstaining and no votes recorded in opposition. Vice chair, secretary, and a replacement public records officer were all left unfilled, with the incoming chair stating she wanted to define the records role before asking anyone to take it. During public comment a citizen asserted the chair election was roughly five months overdue, saying the Commission’s own procedures and policy called for it in April and that the February through May minutes contain no mention of an election; the chair acknowledged the point and committed to keeping to the calendar going forward. This site reports only what the recording establishes: the resignation stated at this meeting is from the Solid Waste Disposal Commission. Whether it affects any other office he holds is not established by this record and is not claimed here.

How to Engage

Concrete steps for participating in the public process. None of these require special expertise or membership.

Attend Public Meetings

  1. Find the meeting. LCSWDC has met on the third Thursday at 6:00 PM, but the date is not always announced in advance. Check the county events calendar. Written comment may also be sent to LCSWDC.Ross@gmail.com before a meeting.
  2. Arrive early. Meetings are open to the public. Sign up to speak if public comment is offered.
  3. Ask specific questions. Reference permit numbers, document dates, or specific contract clauses. General complaints are less effective than targeted questions.
  4. Document the response. Take notes on what officials say. Follow up in writing if commitments are made.

File Public Records Requests

  1. Use the generator above to draft a Tennessee Public Records Act request (T.C.A. § 10-7-503).
  2. Submit to the correct custodian. TDEC records go to the Division of Solid Waste Management. County records go to the Loudon County Mayor's office.
  3. Request electronic format. PDFs are faster and cheaper than paper copies.
  4. Follow up. Tennessee law requires a response within 7 business days. If denied, request a written citation of the specific exemption.

Contact Elected Officials

  1. Call before emailing. Phone calls are harder to ignore than emails and create a direct conversation.
  2. Be specific. Reference permit SNL530000203, the 2022 Operating Agreement, or specific inspection dates. Vague messages get form responses.
  3. Ask for action, not awareness. Instead of "I'm concerned," say "I request that you [specific action]."
  4. Follow up in writing. Summarize phone conversations in an email to create a paper trail.

Submit Public Comment

  1. Monitor permit status. Call TDEC at (615) 532-0780 and ask about the current status of Permit SNL530000203 and whether a public comment period is open.
  2. Submit written comment. When a comment period is open, submit written comments referencing specific permit conditions or regulatory standards.
  3. Request a hearing. If a formal hearing is not scheduled, you can request one through TDEC's administrative procedures.
  4. Share findings. If you obtain records through FOIA, consider sharing them so other residents can build on your work.
