If your septic system has failed or your well has gone dry, Communities Unlimited has septic system financing up to $15,000 at a 1% fixed rate, with up to 20 years to pay it back. It covers repairing or replacing a household septic system or water well at a home you already own. The money comes through a U.S. Department of Agriculture program, so there is an income limit, and it is worth checking that first.
A 1% rate is not a promotion. CU is a Community Development Financial Institution, or CDFI, which means we lend where banks usually will not, and our loans for septic systems are funded by USDA to reach rural households that cannot cover a $12,000 septic replacement out of pocket.
Most financing for septic systems and water wells is a personal loan somewhere between 8% and 36%. This is not that.
Individuals in the Communities Unlimited and Midwest Assistance Program service areas are eligible to apply for the septic & well loan program.
Eligible states:
Alabama, Arkansas, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, and Wyoming
Do I qualify?
Four rules, and all four have to be true.
Your home is in a rural community of 50,000 people or fewer.
Most of the towns CU works in are well under that. If you are not sure, ask us.
You own and live in the home, or you are in the process of buying it.
Renters are not eligible for this program. If you rent, call us anyway, there may be another
route.
The work is on an existing home, not new construction.
You can put in a brand-new septic system or drill a brand-new well. What you cannot do is use
this loan for a house that is still being built.
Your household income is at or under 60% of your state’s median non-metropolitan household
income.
USDA sets the limit, not CU, and it counts the income of everyone living in the home who is over 18. The dollar figure is
different in every state.
Add up the income of everyone in the home who is over 18. If the total is at or under the limit for your state, you qualify on income.
USDA sets these limits and they differ by state. The right-hand column is your household income limit.

What septic tank financing covers
Septic work:
Replacing a failed septic tank
Replacing or repairing a drain field
Repairing a system that is backing up or surfacing
Installing a septic system at a home that has never had one
Pumping, inspection and permit costs tied to the work
Well work:
Drilling a new household water well
Deepening or re-drilling a well that has gone dry
Replacing a failed pump, pressure tank or well casing
Repairing a well that has tested unsafe
Water well financing and well drilling costs
The same loan covers wells. If you searched for water well financing or well drilling financing and landed on lender comparison pages, this is the low-rate option those pages leave out. Up to $15,000 at 1% covers most household well work outright, including a full re-drill in most of the states we lend in.

How To Apply
1. Check the income limit for your state in the table above.
2. Send us your name, phone and state using the form on this page.
3. We call you. Alexandria Cordero is the loan officer for this program. She will walk through what your system needs and what documents to gather.
4. Send in income verification and a bid for the work. A contractor’s written estimate is what sets the loan amount.
5. Close and schedule the work.
If the income limit rules you out
The income limit on this program is set by USDA and CU cannot waive it. If you are over the line, you are not out of options.
USDA Section 504 Home Repair loans and grants. Loans for rural homeowners with income under 50% of area median, and grants for homeowners 62 and older. Different program, different limits, and septic and well work qualifies. Start with your state’s USDA Rural Development office.
Your state health department or county environmental office. Several states run their own septic repair or replacement funds, and some counties have grant money for failing systems that threaten groundwater.
Your state’s Clean Water State Revolving Fund. A handful of states pass this money down to homeowners for decentralized septic work through a local sponsor.
CU’s other lending. If your income is above the USDA limit, we may still be able to lend for home repairs through a different CU program. Ask.
The free well and septic check, regardless of income. In our seven states, we will look at your system at no charge and tell you honestly what it needs. That report is what a contractor bid and any funding application gets built on.
Not sure where to start? Call us at (479) 443-2700. Pointing people to the right money is part of the work, whether or not the loan comes from us.
Septic Tank Financing FAQ
Can you finance a septic system?
Yes. CU lends up to $15,000 at 1% fixed for up to 20 years to repair or replace a household septic system, if your home is in a rural community under 50,000 people and your household income is at or under the limit for your state. See the chart above.
What are the income limits?
Your household income has to be at or under 60% of the median non-metropolitan household income for your state. USDA sets that figure and it differs by state. See the table above for your state. Everyone in the home over 18 counts toward the total.
Do you check credit?
This is not a no-credit-check loan, but it is not a credit-score product either. Eligibility turns on your household income, where you live, and whether you own the home. We look at whether you can repay, and at 1% over 20 years the payment on a $15,000 loan is small.
How much can I borrow?
Up to $15,000. A written estimate from your contractor sets the actual amount.
Are there grants for septic systems instead of loans?
Sometimes, though there are far fewer than people expect and most carry tighter income rules than this loan. USDA Section 504 has grants for rural homeowners 62 and older. Some state health departments and counties have septic repair funds. If a grant fits your situation better, we will say so.
Can I use this for a water well?
Yes. Drilling a new household well, deepening one that has gone dry, or replacing a pump, pressure tank or casing all qualify.
Can I use it on a new house I am building?
No. The work has to be on a home that already exists. You can install a septic system or well where there has never been one, as long as the house is already there.
What if I rent?
This program requires that you own and live in the home, or are in the process of buying it. Call us anyway at (479) 443-2700 and we will point you toward something that fits.
